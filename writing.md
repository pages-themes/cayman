  <h3>📝 What Red-Teaming AI Taught Me About Safety</h3>
  <p><em>Status: Outline</em></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hook:</strong> "Before you ship that model, try to break it. I mean really break it."</li>
    <li><strong>Core idea:</strong> Red-teaming isn't just finding bad outputs — it's building a systematic framework for understanding *how* a model fails and *who* it fails for. Drawing on CAMLIS and Bias Bounty experience.</li>
    <li><strong>Structure:</strong> What red-teaming actually looks like (not what people think) → methodology I used → failure patterns I found → what model developers should do with this information → why this matters more than benchmarks</li>
    <li><strong>Audience:</strong> AI practitioners, safety researchers, and hiring managers at frontier labs</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>📝 From Wargames to AI Evaluations</h3>
  <p><em>Status: Outline</em></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hook:</strong> "The Army taught me to stress-test decisions before lives depended on them. Now I do the same thing with AI systems."</li>
    <li><strong>Core idea:</strong> Operations research and military wargaming are natural precursors to AI evaluation. Both ask: what happens when this system encounters conditions it wasn't designed for?</li>
    <li><strong>Structure:</strong> What wargaming actually is (analytical studies, simulations, adversarial testing) → direct parallels to AI eval (red-teaming, stress testing, edge cases) → what frontier labs can learn from how the military evaluates decision systems → my path from DoD analyst to AI evaluation</li>
    <li><strong>Audience:</strong> AI safety community, ops research folks curious about AI, hiring managers</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>📝 Building AI for People Who Weren't in the Room</h3>
  <p><em>Status: Outline</em></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Hook:</strong> "I grew up in Ecuador. My family moved to New Jersey. At every step, someone told me I didn't belong. Now I build AI systems — and I think about who's not in the room every single day."</li>
    <li><strong>Core idea:</strong> The personal mission statement, expanded. Connecting the immigrant journey and military experience to why inclusive AI design isn't a nice-to-have — it's an engineering requirement.</li>
    <li><strong>Structure:</strong> Personal story (brief) → what "not in the room" means concretely for AI (data representation, evaluation demographics, deployment contexts) → examples from bias bounty and nonprofit work → what "helpful, honest, and harmless" actually requires</li>
    <li><strong>Audience:</strong> Broad — AI practitioners, DEI advocates, anyone thinking about who AI serves</li>
  </ul>
